Spesa splits receipts and shared expenses between friends, live, on a phone. The interface is a calm, paper-and-ink ledger: warm off-white ground, one deep green and its partner red for money in and out, a rust kept for errors, a serif for headings and a plain sans for everything else. Build every screen to feel like a well-kept notebook, not a banking app.

## Content fundamentals

- **Voice: a friend who's good with money.** Plain, warm, direct. Second person ("you"), people by first name. "You owe Marta €12.40", "Marta owes you €8.00", "You're all settled up in this group."
- **Sentence case everywhere** — buttons, titles, tabs: "Settle up", "Record payment", "Create a new group". Never ALL CAPS except the `micro-caps` stats label.
- **Buttons are verbs** naming the outcome: "Save bill", "Leave group", "Delete all bills" — never "OK" or "Submit".
- **Empty states say what to do next, and offer it** (the `EmptyState` card): a short title ("No bills yet", "Everyone's even"), one line of why or what next ("Add the first one, then scan its receipt or type the items in."), and a button that takes you there. Never "above" or "below": the button is the direction.
- **Errors explain and offer a way out**: what failed, then the fallback.
- **Money**: always with its currency symbol, two decimals, in `ink` and 600 weight when it is the headline figure. Displayed amounts use the `mono` face (`.mono`), so digits line up in columns; a number that stays in the body face and sits in a column (editable price, quantity and payer fields, the "you owe" line under a bill total) gets `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`. Balances come in pairs and are treated alike: "you owe / you borrowed" in `negative`, "owes you / you lent" in `accent-dark` — red out, green in — and the amount is always 600, whether the person colours the whole balance line or only the amount (Settings > Layout, per device). Never a sign ("−€12.40"): the words and the colour already say it.
- **"Spent" means your share** (what you consumed), not what you fronted for the whole bill; say "fronted" explicitly when that is the figure.
- Em dashes and "·" separators are house style ("Paid by Marta · 6 items"). No emoji in UI chrome.

## Visual foundations

**Colour.** Neutral-first. `bg` is the page, `surface` raises modals, sheets, inputs and tiles, and `surface-tint` (a whisper of green) is reserved for list rows. Text is `ink`, secondary text `ink-soft`. `accent` is the one brand hue: primary buttons, active states, focus, bar fills. Use `accent-dark` whenever the accent is *text* (links, labels on `accent-light`). `negative` is money going out (debts, spending up, over budget), the partner of `accent-dark`'s green. `warn` + `warn-light` carry errors and destruction only — a rust, never decoration. At AAA lightness two reds can't be told apart by hue (~7 apart in light, ~5 in dark), so errors are marked by form as well: every `.status-error` carries the warning triangle and a box, destructive actions are filled buttons or iconned menu items. A debt is never boxed, an error never bare text beside an amount. Dark mode is a first-class theme (`[data-theme="dark"]` on `<html>`, with a System option that follows the OS), down to the phone's status bar: `browser-bar` is the brand green in light and the page's own near-black in dark, and it follows the theme shown, not just the OS. Every colour token has a dark value: the surfaces sit close to near-black, `accent`, `negative` and `warn` brighten, their labels flip to near-black, and `accent-dark` inverts to a light mint.

**Category colours** (`category-1` … `category-7`) are data colours for dots, pie slices and bars only — never text or UI chrome — and always sit next to the category's name. One per default category, in slot order: Groceries indigo, Eating out orange, Household green, Bills & utilities pink, Transport blue, Health red, Other grey (also uncategorized); plus a custom colour picker. Store the preset hex on the category, but paint through `categoryColor()`, which returns `var(--category-N)` so the colour follows the theme and the colour-blind palette; custom colours show exactly as saved. The default palette clears 3:1 on every surface in both themes and keeps any two categories clearly apart for full colour vision. The opt-in **colour-blind palette** (`category-cb-*`, Settings > Layout, per device) re-steps the same families in lightness so any two stay apart under red-green colour blindness. No palette makes seven categories identifiable by colour alone for everyone, so never show a category colour without its name, and never rely on a tooltip for it: a bill's items show it on a small `ink-soft` line under each item (the default) or, per device in Settings > Layout, grouped under one heading per category with its subtotal (`ItemRow`). A pie chart names its slices without asking anyone to match colours by eye: slices of 6% or more print their percent just outside the ring in `ink-soft` (never text on the slice itself, which can't reach 7:1), the legend beside it carries the same percent, and hovering, focusing or tapping a slice or its legend row highlights both (an `ink` outline on the slice, a `border` outline and 600 name on the row) while the centre of the donut names that category with its amount and share.

**Type.** Three families. `display` (Fraunces, 500–600) for page titles, section heads, sheet titles, month dividers and the wordmark. `body` (Inter 400/500/600) for everything else. `mono` (IBM Plex Mono 400/500/600) for displayed amounts, day dividers and codes/keys. Every weight a style asks for must be a loaded one, or the browser fakes the bold. The fonts are self-hosted, never loaded from a third-party CDN: the app ships Latin and Latin-ext woff2 subsets (`src/fonts.css`, split by `unicode-range` so accented Central European letters load only when a page uses them) and its service worker precaches them, so the installed app keeps its typefaces offline. This system carries the Latin files (`fonts/`); all three families are under the SIL Open Font License (`licenses/OFL.txt`). The scale is small and phone-first, eleven named steps (`text-2xs` … `text-5xl`): 22 page title · 18 sheet title · 16 section · 15 body/button · 14 secondary · 13 label · 12 caption · 11 micro, plus 20 for stat figures and 28/34 for the wordmark and mark. The steps are in rem, never px, so the whole app grows with the browser's own text-size setting; use a `text-*` token, never a literal size. Inputs are always `text-lg` (16px at the default size).

**Spacing.** Four steps: `space-xs` 6 · `space-sm` 10 · `space-md` 16 · `space-lg` 24. Pages are a 560px column (wider only where **Layout** says) with `space-md` side padding and `space-lg` top. Lists stack with `space-sm` between rows; sections separate with `space-lg` and a 1px `border` hairline above every section after the first.

**Shape.** One main radius, `radius` (12px), for buttons, cards, tiles and modals; `radius-xs` (4px) for code chips; `radius-sm` (8px) for inputs and icon buttons; `radius-md` (10px) for dropdown menus, the tab track and swipe rows; `radius-lg` (14px) for the app mark; `radius-xl` (20px) for the auth card and sheet tops; pills (`radius-pill`) for chips and bars; circles (50%) for avatars and submit buttons.

**Stacking.** Four named layers, lowest to highest: `z-sticky` (the pagination bar), `z-popover` (dropdown menus and pickers), `z-floating` (the install banner), `z-overlay` (modal and sheet backdrops). Anything new joins one of them; never a literal z-index.

**Borders over shadows.** Surfaces separate by a 1px outline, not by elevation or a change of fill alone, in two strengths. `border` is the quiet line (about 2:1, in the palette's own green-grey) around list rows, cards, tiles, menus, tables and dividers: their content already says what they are, so the outline only needs to be there, not to compete with it. `border-strong` (3:1) is for outlines that *are* the control, where finding the edge matters: text and date fields, dropdowns, the note box, and the switch and slider tracks. Never use `border-strong` for a card, or `border` for a field. Shadows are rare: `shadow-tab` on the active tab, `shadow-popover` on dropdown menus and the sticky pagination bar, `shadow-floating` on the install banner only. They're soft in light and much stronger in dark, where a light shadow vanishes on the near-black page; always use the tokens, never a literal shadow.

**States.** Hover/press on rows: `accent` border, pressed adds `accent-light`. Buttons press to `scale(0.98)`; disabled drops to 0.55 opacity. **Focus:** every control — buttons, links, chips, avatars, tabs, list rows — shows the same keyboard focus ring: a solid 2px `accent` outline, 2px outside the element, on `:focus-visible` only, so a tap or click leaves no ring behind. `accent` is at least 4.5:1 on every surface in both themes. Inside anything that clips its overflow (swipe-to-delete rows, the collapsible bill summary, scrolling modals and lists) the ring moves 2px *inside* the element so it isn't cut off. Text fields ring on every focus, tap included (1px offset), because a field being typed in should always say so. Never remove the outline without putting a visible replacement in its place: a borderless field rings its container (`:focus-within`), a custom slider rings its thumb. Submit circles are invisible until their field has content, then fade in over 0.22s.

**Touch targets.** Every control is at least 44×44px to a fingertip (WCAG 2.5.5, the AAA target), whether or not it looks it. Small controls — icon buttons, text links, tabs, submit circles, dismiss "×", the account chip, avatars — keep their look and get an invisible 44px tap area (`::after`) centred on them; links and tabs grow vertically only, since they sit side by side. Where neighbours are too close for that, the controls themselves get the room: split-with avatars are 36px with an 8px gap (a 44px pitch — the gap is a deliberate literal, not a spacing step), and the Settings avatar sizes are 36 / 42 / 48px. When a row can't fit 44px targets on a 360px phone (the week view's six time-range arrows), keep them 44px tall and as wide as fits, never overlapping.

**Loading and empty.** While a page or section loads, show a faint outline of what's coming (`Skeleton`), never a bare "Loading…": placeholder shapes sized like the real rows, tiles and bars, so nothing jumps when the data lands. The shapes are `ink-soft` at 20%, pulse gently (still under `prefers-reduced-motion`), and appear only after 300ms, so a fast load never flashes one; the label ("Loading your groups…") is announced at once for screen readers. Never show an empty state or a zero while the data is still coming: "No stats yet" to someone with stats is worse than a placeholder. When there really is nothing, the `EmptyState` card: `accent-dark` icon on an `accent-light` circle, a Fraunces 600 title, one `ink-soft` line and the next step, in a dashed `border` frame so it never reads as a real row. The button is `btn-primary` when it starts something ("Create a group", "Add a bill": each just focuses the field that does it) and `btn-secondary` when it only takes you somewhere ("Back to the group", "Settle up", "Clear filters"). It never starts a new flow of its own.

**Motion.** Short and functional: 0.05–0.22s eases, sheets slide up in 0.18s, the boot mark "breathes" at 1.8s. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.

**Layout.** Mobile-first PWA, 360–430px phones are the target; the app never scrolls sideways (long titles wrap anywhere). Safe-area insets pad the body and bottom sheets. Most pages stay the 560px reading column on every screen, since forms and lists read worse stretched; two use a tablet's or laptop's room. From 768px the stats and graphs pages widen to 1000px and set their sections two to a row (a hairline above each row after the first, none beside); the line chart stops growing at 320px tall and the pie legend at 440px wide. From 1024px the group page keeps its bill list in a left pane (360–440px, a `border` hairline on its right) and opens a bill in the right pane instead of replacing the list, each pane scrolling on its own under the app header; the open bill's row is marked like a pressed row plus a 2px `accent` edge, and with no bill open the right pane is an `EmptyState` ("No bill open"). The app header lines up with the frame below it.

**Contrast: AAA in both themes.** Every text colour (`ink`, `ink-soft`, `accent-dark`, `negative`, `warn`) is at least 7:1 on every ground it can land on: `bg`, `surface`, `surface-tint` and `accent-light` (hover and focused rows, secondary buttons, success messages), and `warn-light` for `warn`. Labels on fills use `on-accent` and `on-warn` (7:1 or better). They're white in light and near-black in dark, where the green and rust are brighter, so never paint a literal white on a fill. `border-strong` (field and control outlines) is at least 3:1 on every surface, the quiet `border` at least 1.5:1, and the `accent` focus ring and bar fills at least 4.5:1. Before adding a new text/ground pairing, check it against this. The app enforces all of it in a unit test (`src/lib/contrast.test.js`) that reads the tokens straight from the stylesheet, both themes and both category palettes, and fails the build if any pair drops below its line; add a new token or pairing to that test when you add it. Printed recaps follow the same rule on paper: every print text colour is at least 7:1 on white. Category colours meet 3:1 as marks in both themes; custom colours people pick are the one exception, and they're always shown beside their label.

**State is never shade or opacity alone.** The active tab has an outline and 600 weight; people who've left a group are marked by a dashed border at full opacity, not by fading; status messages carry a border in their own ink, and errors a warning icon; placeholders are `ink-soft`. Only disabled controls fade (0.55).


## Where this lives, and what's the source of truth

- **The app's stylesheet is the truth.** Every token here is a CSS custom property in `src/styles/tokens.css` of [doc-98/expense-splitter](https://github.com/Doc-98/expense-splitter), under the same name (`bg` is `--bg`, `text-md` is `--text-md`), and the components are the app's own classes in `src/styles/**`. If this system and the app ever disagree, the app wins and this gets fixed.
- **Two identical copies of this system:** this page (the browsable one, with live previews) and `docs/design-system/` in the repository (the same brand book, `tokens.json`, component cards, previews and `bundle.css` as plain files, for anyone who can't open this page). A unit test, `src/lib/designSystemTokens.test.js`, fails the build if `tokens.json` and `tokens.css` disagree on a value, or if a token exists in one and not the other.
- **Files named here that live elsewhere in the repo:** the fonts are in `src/assets/fonts/` (declared in `src/fonts.css`), the icons in `src/components/icons.jsx`, and the app mark and PWA icons are `public/favicon.svg`, `public/icon-*.png` and `public/apple-touch-icon.png`.
- **Changing the design:** change the app first, then mirror the change here and in `docs/design-system/` in the same piece of work: tokens, the component's card and preview, `bundle.css`, and this brand book when a rule changes. Check any new text/ground pair against the contrast rules above; `src/lib/contrast.test.js` enforces them.

## Iconography

A small hand-drawn set of 31 inline SVG icons (`assets/Icons`): 24×24 viewBox, 2px round-capped strokes, occasional 35%-opacity fills, drawn to `currentColor` so they inherit the button's colour. Default size 20px (16px inside buttons). They are always `aria-hidden` — the button carries the label. Don't mix in an icon library; draw new glyphs to the same grid and stroke. No emoji as icons.

## Logo

There is no wordmark file: the brand is the name "Spesa" set in Fraunces 600 (`brand` / `header-brand` styles) in `accent-dark`. The app mark (`assets/Logos`) is the Fraunces 600 "S", outlined as a path so it never depends on a font, in `bg` on an `accent` square: rounded (`radius-lg`, 14/64) for the favicon and the standard app icon, full-bleed for the maskable icon (launchers crop it to their own shape) and the iPhone home-screen icon (iOS rounds it itself). The "S" is 34/64 of the square's size in font units, centred on its own ink, the same proportions as the on-screen boot mark.
